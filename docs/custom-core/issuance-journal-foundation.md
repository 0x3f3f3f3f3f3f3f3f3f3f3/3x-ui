# Issuance journal foundation

This is the Task 3 control-plane primitive under [restore-authority-lifecycle-plan.md](restore-authority-lifecycle-plan.md). It is not yet used to authorize business listeners. Existing managed remote-scope guards remain in force.

`internal/policyauthority` explicitly creates or opens one private synchronous bbolt journal outside imported SQL and node execution state. Creation refuses an existing file; opening refuses missing, wrong-identity, shared-permission or contradictory state. Initial seeds remain immutable. Confirmed raw/billed history, initial and per-grant fractions, current window use, uncertain frozen allowance, outstanding finite capacity and directional rate/burst sums survive reopen. Opening recomputes cumulative history and held allocation sums from the complete records.

Issuance binds authority identity/generation, node/source/fresh boot, canonical client, window/version and request/challenge. A transaction commits the finite allocation before returning it. Exact request retries reuse the committed grant; changed intent is rejected. Limited zero directional shares are distinct from explicit unlimited mode. Independently issued capacity and limited rate/burst sums cannot exceed their policy bounds. Reported billed consumption is accumulated directly, without applying a multiplier again. Cumulative reports reject regression, conflicting sequence reuse and capacity excess.

A current-incarnation seal releases only verified unused capacity and its shares. Node reboot, expiry, lost control, deletion or an unconfirmed report releases nothing. Retired boot identities are durably recorded and cannot be reactivated by delayed registration. Deleted canonical identities retain terminal tombstones and old allocation/history. Exact provisioning retries preserve the original seed and current confirmed totals.

Schema 2 adds immutable policy/window transition records and a per-account revision. Exact change/reset retries preserve the first captured boundary, including after reopen and later reports. Reset deliberately credits confirmed period usage and initial frozen uncertainty, while retaining outstanding grants and lifetime evidence. Late reports after the boundary enter the current window. Policy decreases retain old quota/rate/burst holds; an old unlimited directional grant also blocks new limited allocation until sealed. A prior window identifier cannot be reused. Historical grants remain tied to their original policy.

Confirmed and held billed quantities use whole bytes plus millionths, with exact carry/borrow across grants, resets and reopen. Settlement never applies the multiplier again. Fractional usage cannot regress or exceed its finite grant, and sealing cannot round remaining capacity up. Every committed effect advances a signed-SQL-compatible revision once; retries do not. Opening recomputes revisions, policy history, cumulative fractions and remaining capacity. Earlier schema-1 development journals are rejected and retained for explicit reconciliation; missing history is never silently initialized.

The conservative allocator retains outstanding quota until an authenticated durable seal. Expiration alone does not reclaim billed capacity. A durably replaced boot cannot renew; its directional rate/burst shares may be released only after the maximum ten-second execution lease has elapsed on the authority process's monotonic clock. Reopening the authority restarts the full quarantine for unreleased retired shares. The journal records this retirement separately from a seal and recomputes its account revision and rate sums when reopened. Confirmed use, fractions and unsealed capacity remain held. This trades availability for exclusion; a private controller must renew or replace execution grants while retaining the authority's quota holds.

Configured core construction generates a fresh 128-bit boot nonce before opening recovered accounts or arming expiry callbacks. Capabilities add protobuf field 7 (`boot_id`) and advertise `boot-bound-execution-grants-v1` only on a configured persistent instance. Private Unix RPCs bind the authority, obtain monotonic challenges, install finite grants, checkpoint their cumulative exact usage and seal them. A grant matches authority/generation/node, source, boot, client, policy version and quota window. Installation persists its accounting boundary before enabling admission. Duplicates preserve the original deadline, capacity and limiter tokens; retired grant sequences cannot reactivate. Fresh boots do not activate stored grants or seal a previous incarnation's capacity.

Admission enforces the finite billed capacity and explicit directional shares, including unavailable zero shares. Lease loss or expiry closes applicable sessions and rejects reconnection. Fresh monotonic challenges can renew an existing grant without issuing capacity, repricing usage or refilling limiter tokens; exact renewal retries retain the original deadline. Expired or superseded grants cannot renew. An explicit durable handoff seals the old grant and pauses traffic until its replacement arrives; the old deadline still closes it on failure. Pending connection registrations also wait during this controlled handoff, limited to 128 per client and bounded by the old deadline/cancellation. Ordinary sealed or missing authority remains a rejection. Sealing commits final spent capacity and exact counters before acknowledging unused capacity. Storage failure exposes no successful seal and closes admission. TCP readers/writers and SSH reverse writers segment streams by the assigned burst and affordable remaining capacity; UDP packets retain their original boundary. The panel adapter requires the negotiated fresh boot and grant capabilities before sending these calls.

Seventy engine/command/dispatcher parents passed under race; eight SSH parents passed under race with local test listeners. A real private-RPC TCP/UDP Tunnel test delivered 320 bytes in each direction, billed 960 bytes at multiplier 1.5, preserved UDP boundaries and rejected reconnection after exhausting its grant. The configured copied-state regression now passes by rejecting admission without a fresh grant. Additional RED→GREEN evidence covers replay, exact seal retries/failure, delayed challenges, small stream bursts and the constructor/expiry nonce race. These bounded checks do not yet establish coordinated native protocol or node activation. Renewal and bounded policy-transition continuity now pass their executor and private RPC gates. Actual managed runtime bootstrap and issuer settlement remain open, and the newly gated source is not ready for publication.

The journal assumes one active control authority. Its file lock excludes a second open of the same file; it cannot exclude a simultaneously operated copy of the whole authority or prevent authority-state rollback. Those cases require an external fencing source. Copied node SQL/execution snapshots must be fenced by fresh core incarnations and this unchanged authority; that integration remains open.

Twenty-five top-level journal race tests passed in `restore-authority-journal-projection-precommit.jsonl` (1.925 seconds); package vet and diff checks passed. They include the opening, issuance, settlement and tombstone regressions, durable transition/retry boundaries, lowered limits, unlimited-to-limited exclusion, initial uncertainty reset, exact fractional carry and SQL-limit transaction rollback. Deleted identities reject even exact replay of previously issued unsealed grants. Opening also refuses to recreate a file that disappears after the initial path check, and enforces the journal size limit through bbolt's open options. These tests prove the journal's bounds, not encrypted protocol payload or coordinated node enforcement.

`client_policy_authority_projections` stores the canonical identity, authority identity/generation, revision and exact account snapshot. The private service adapter joins the existing serialized SQL transaction and restore lease/generation checks before calling the journal. Contradictory identity, future revision or same-revision payload fails before issuance. Journal issuance commits before projection; SQL failure exposes no grant and retains the original durable allocation for retry. A valid older projection can be rebuilt from the journal. Terminal deletion also restores the SQL tombstone without erasing final usage.

Six SQLite authority integration parents passed under race (5.192 seconds), including actual online-backup/reopen of an older SQL snapshot with the journal retained. Private grant execution is implemented, but initial authority migration and managed activation still need to install and settle these grants before business traffic can run.

Isolated endpoint fixtures explicitly supply finite test grants and sealed policy handoffs; they do not claim journal allocation or authenticated multi-node activation.

The private service execution bridge now negotiates and binds an actual core boot, durably registers it, commits and projects an allocation before installing it, and settles exact per-grant cumulative receipts. Its network calls run outside the serialized SQL transaction. Lost SQL or core replies retain the committed allocation; retries reuse the original request and challenge. Renewal retries retain their prepared challenge and duration, preserving the original monotonic deadline rather than extending it. Each renewal rechecks the current boot, policy/window and SQL projection before mutating the core; policy changes and window resets prevent renewal of superseded grants while allowing their final accounting receipts.

Native protocol enforcement code is unchanged in this checkpoint. Journal schema 3 records rate retirement; older foundation schemas are rejected and preserved for explicit migration rather than silently recreated. There is no activated production authority in this development stage.

Managed Runtime startup now offers an authorization callback after client initialization and before the process adds business listeners. It runs outside the Runtime RPC mutex; its API connection is temporary and must not be retained after the callback. The explicit-journal acceptance fixture installs an allocation through the service bridge in that callback, then proves actual Tunnel payload and exact settlement. A projection failure stops the core, leaves the business port closed and retains the journal allocation. The functional missing-callback regression and correction are retained in `restore-authority-bootstrap-hook-{red,first-green}.log`; the combined startup/deletion/stale-candidate race gate passed (Runtime 1.846 seconds, service 4.174 seconds). The ordinary bootstrap factory still requires the explicit migration and controller integration before it can supply this callback in production.

These tests also reproduced and fixed an empty-read ownership race in the dispatcher: interruption released a multi-buffer still returned to the caller. Empty input is now released before returning no payload. Native quota checks preserve their original counters/thresholds and assert the exact combined quota and grant exhaustion flags. A final admitted TCP buffer can be interrupted when the exact allowance closes its owner; positive handoff delivery is measured before exhaustion, and exact admission plus reconnect denial is checked separately. The core's admitted counters are not presented as confirmed endpoint delivery.
